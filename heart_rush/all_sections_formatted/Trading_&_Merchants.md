# Trade Routes & Merchants

Not all wealth comes from delving into dungeons or completing heroic quests. For those with a keen eye for opportunity and a talent for negotiation, establishing trade routes can provide steady income that grows your wealth far beyond what any single treasure hoard might offer.

## Creating Trade Routes

A trade route is a regular circuit where goods are bought in one location and sold in another for profit. Setting one up is a **Stratagem** of 2 to 5 objectives (see the Stratagem System section), and the GM states how many at the start.

Each objective is one concrete step, such as signing a supplier in the eastern ports, winning a buyer in the capital, or getting the permit to sell in the capital's market. The GM sets how long each roll takes; setting up a whole route typically takes several weeks to months.

### Going Yourself

If you travel the route, negotiate with merchants, and oversee the first shipment, you play the objectives as scenes. Use the Journeys section for travel, and play out the negotiations.

A scene that goes well finishes its objective with no roll. A scene that goes badly costs what a scene costs: the GM may add a hindrance, or put a penalty on your next roll.

### Sending Others

If employees or partners set up the route, you roll for each objective. The roll is your Heart die + your Presence or Cunning die plus the usual profession bonus, against the setup CN. You can still go yourself for any single objective and play it as a scene instead of rolling it.

A failed roll costs the time it covered, and the GM decides any further cost when it happens. The failed objective stays open. If the failure closes off the way you meant to finish it (the buyer is gone), the GM replaces the objective only when another way plausibly exists.

A 1 on either die gives the usual complication choice, as described in The Complication Rule (Dice Mechanics and Resolution System). If you take the complication and the objective succeeds, the route gains a hindrance or a running cost (see Complications & Hindrances).

### Setup CN

The GM sets the setup CN from how hard the whole route is to establish, given the situation as it stands. Everything you already have is counted in this number. Do not list it as an asset, and do not list what is already in the way as a hindrance.

**Already in hand (these lower the setup CN)**:

- Existing merchant contacts in target cities
- Strong reputation in the region
- Letters of introduction from influential figures
- Experienced trade factor handling negotiations
- Knowledge of local customs and languages
- Previous successful routes in the area

**Already in the way (these raise the setup CN)**:

- Established competitors controlling the market
- Political tensions between regions
- Language and cultural barriers
- Suspicious local authorities
- Recent bandit activity on the route
- Trading in restricted or unusual goods

Only changes after setup become assets and hindrances, and each one changes the CN by 3.

### When the Route Is Established

Finishing the last objective establishes the route. The GM sets its route difficulty (see Route Difficulty) and its **market size** at setup.

The market size is the most money the two ends of the route can take in a season, set from how big those markets are. Money you invest above it earns no profit.

## Operating Established Routes

Once established, routes run each season: you choose how the goods travel, roll once, and collect profit.

### Route Difficulty

After establishment, the GM sets the route's difficulty:

| Route Conditions  | Base CN |
| ----------------- | ------- |
| Safe & Profitable | 6       |
| Moderate Risk     | 9       |
| Risky             | 12      |
| Dangerous         | 15      |
| Desperate         | 18      |

This number stays the same until circumstances change (new assets or hindrances emerge). The goods and the shipping choice, below, add to it each season.

### Goods

The kind of goods sets the profit and adds to the route's CN. Both are fixed.

| Goods                                    | Profit | CN  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------ | --- |
| **Staple** (grain, ore, timber)          | 20%    | +0  |
| **Crafted** (tools, weapons, cloth)      | 40%    | +3  |
| **Luxury** (spices, silk, jewelry)       | 80%    | +6  |
| **Exotic** (magic items, rare artifacts) | 160%   | +9  |

Profit is a percentage of the money you invest in the season's cargo, up to the route's market size.

### Shipping Choice

Each season, before stating the CN, the GM asks how the cargo travels. You make one choice, and it applies to all your routes.

- **Cheap**: +3 CN, profit ×1.5. Minimal guards, exposed caravans, cargo space on a rough ship.
- **Standard**: no change. Normal merchant caravans and typical shipping.
- **Secure**: −3 CN, profit ×0.5. Private airship, teleportation circles, heavy military escort.

Operational expenses are assumed to be covered by the profit unless extreme (hiring mercenary companies, magical transportation, operating in active war zones). Negotiate those with the GM.

### Seasonal Trade Roll

Once per season, for all your routes together:

1. Choose the shipping
2. The GM states the CN of each route: base CN + goods + shipping, +3 for each hindrance on it and −3 for each asset on it. In trade, an asset or hindrance belongs to the route it touches and changes only that route's CN
3. The roll is against the hardest route's CN, after those changes, plus the penalty for managing many routes (see Managing Multiple Routes)
4. Roll your Heart die + Presence or Cunning die, plus the usual profession bonus

**Success**: Every route completes and earns its profit.  
**Failure**: Roll on the Failure Table for one route, chosen by the GM. The other routes complete and earn their profit.  
**Complication** (1 on either die): As described in The Complication Rule (Dice Mechanics and Resolution System), you choose between failing and rerolling that die. If the new total succeeds, the routes complete and one route, chosen by the GM, gains a hindrance or a running cost, or starts a glut or shortage. If the new total still fails, roll on the Failure Table for one route and apply the complication to one route; the GM chooses each route.

### Calculating Investment & Profit

**Investment**: The cost of goods at purchase price, up to the route's market size.

**Profit**: Investment × the goods percentage × the shipping multiplier. A glut halves it, a shortage doubles it, and each running cost subtracts its fixed amount.

### Glut & Shortage

A **glut** halves a route's profit. A **shortage** doubles it.

A glut or shortage on a route starts from a complication, a failure result, a scene, or a world event the GM announces. It lasts until a scene or the story changes it.

### Failure Table (d10)

1-2. **Total Loss**: Caravan/ship lost entirely—lose all investment  
3-4. **Partial Loss**: Half cargo lost or spoiled—recover 50% of investment  
5-6. **Break Even**: Delays and fees consume profit—recover investment only  
7-8. **Delayed**: No investment or profit returns this season. The shipment arrives next season and is resolved by next season's roll: if that roll succeeds, the delayed shipment returns its investment and pays its profit along with that season's; if it fails, the delayed shipment's route is the one the GM may pick for the Failure Table  
9. **Reduced Profit**: Forced to sell at poor prices—gain 25% normal profit  
10. **Silver Lining**: Recover the investment, earn no profit, and gain a new asset for the route

### Complications & Hindrances

A complication leaves a lasting problem on one route. It is one of two kinds:

- A **hindrance**: the route's CN goes up by 3.
- A **running cost**: the route's profit is cut by a fixed amount each season. The GM decides the amount when the running cost appears.

A scene that deals with the problem removes it. Examples:

- "Damaged reputation with merchant guild" - Hindrance
- "Rival saboteur in organization" - Hindrance, until discovered
- "Suspicious customs officials" - Hindrance
- "Deteriorating road conditions" - Hindrance
- "Protection fees to local gang" - Running cost
- "Forced to pay guild dues" - Running cost

## Accompanying Routes

You can personally accompany a route's shipment and play it out in scenes instead of rolling for that route. The season's roll then covers your other routes. The accompanied route still counts when determining the penalty for managing 3–4 or 5+ routes.

- Use the Journeys section for travel
- Handle negotiations and dangers directly
- A scene that goes well completes the route for the season. It faces the same situation as the roll would have, and the GM sets its checks from that situation
- Potential to discover new assets, or to remove hindrances and running costs through play


## Managing Multiple Routes

Operating many routes simultaneously creates logistical challenges:

**Personal Management Limits**:

- 1-2 routes: No difficulty
- 3-4 routes: +3 CN to the season's roll
- 5+ routes: +6 CN to the season's roll

**Delegated Management**:

- **Trade Factor**: A trade factor hired after setup is an asset (−3 CN) on the routes they manage, under the normal asset rules. One hired before setup is already counted in the setup CN
- **Merchant House Partnership**: Professional management but takes percentage of profits

## Assets & Improvements

After setup, players can pursue various assets to improve their routes:

**Security Assets**:

- Hired guards for dangerous segments
- Protection agreements with local authorities
- Insurance through merchant guilds
- Armed caravan or naval escort

**Commercial Assets**:

- Exclusive supplier contracts
- Warehouses for storing goods
- Established buyers guaranteeing sales
- Market intelligence networks

**Political Assets**:

- Trading licenses and permits
- Noble or guild patronage
- Diplomatic immunity
- Bribed customs officials

**Operational Assets**:

- Experienced caravan masters
- Faster transportation methods
- Weather prediction capabilities
- Alternative route knowledge

Each asset reduces the CN of the route it applies to by 3, while multiple related assets might eliminate entire categories of risk. An escort that is your shipping choice (the heavy military escort of Secure shipping) is not also an asset.

## Example: The Silk Road

Elena wants to establish a route trading silk between the eastern ports and the capital. Her employees will do the work, so she rolls rather than plays it out.

**Setup**:

- Objectives (3): sign a silk supplier in the eastern ports, win a buyer in the capital, get the guild's permit to sell silk in the capital. Each roll covers one month.
- Setup CN: 12 for each. A competing merchant house and a language barrier push it up. Elena's letter from the Duke and her experienced factor pull it down. All four are already in the situation, so they are counted in the 12 and none is listed as an asset or hindrance.
- Supplier and buyer: both of Elena's rolls succeed.
- Guild permit: her Heart die (d8) shows a 1, so she chooses to reroll it and accept a complication. The reroll is a 5, so the total is 5 + Cunning d8 (5) + Merchant profession (3) = 13. Success.
- Complication: the GM adds a running cost, "Forced to pay guild dues", 3 ducats off each season's profit.

**Route Established**:

- The GM sets route difficulty at Safe & Profitable (6), since both ends are known and the road is quiet.
- The GM sets the market size at 50 ducats.
- Goods: silk is luxury. 80% profit, +6 CN.

**First Season**:

- Elena invests 40 ducats, under the market size, and ships Standard.
- CN: 6 (base) + 6 (luxury) + 0 (standard) = 12
- Roll: Heart die d8 (6) + Cunning d8 (3) + Merchant (3) = 12. Success!
- Profit: 40 × 80% = 32 ducats, minus 3 for the guild dues = 29 ducats.

**Second Season**:

- Elena invests 40 ducats again and chooses Cheap shipping. Cheap adds 3, so the CN is 15, and the profit would be ×1.5.
- Roll: Heart die d8 (5) + Cunning d8 (4) + Merchant (3) = 12. Failure.
- Failure Table d10: 5, Break Even. She recovers her 40 ducats and earns no profit.
- The GM rules that the failed cargo flooded the eastern port with cheap silk. That is a glut on this route: profit is halved until a scene or the story changes it.

Elena considers personally accompanying the next shipment to deal with the guild and the glut. A scene that goes well could remove the guild dues or end the glut.
