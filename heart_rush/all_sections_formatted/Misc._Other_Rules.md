# Misc. Other Rules

A collection of miscellaneous other rules in Heart Rush.

## Learning a Language

Learning a language is a stratagem: a goal too long for one roll, played as a short series of rolls that each cover a stretch of time. The goal is to become fluent, and it has four objectives that you must finish in this order:

1. **Basic phrases**: you understand the most basic words and phrases.
2. **Passable**: you can get by, though slowly and with mistakes.
3. **Conversational**: you can hold an ordinary conversation.
4. **Fluent**: you speak the language as well as a native speaker.

The GM sets the language's difficulty from how complex it is. The difficulty is the time each roll covers: 1 month for an easy language, 3 months for a harder one, 6 months for a very hard one. Learning all four objectives therefore takes at least 4, 12 or 24 months.

Each roll is a Cunning check (Heart die + Cunning die + modifiers). The circumstances under which you are learning set the Challenge Number (CN). Use the following table.

| CN  | Circumstances                                     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 6   | &lt;7 y/o, phenomenal teacher, or complete immersion |
| 9   | Daily lessons from a fluent speaker               |
| 12  | Half-immersion                                    |
| 12  | Self-taught, no interaction with fluent speakers  |

The table sets the CN. When the learner's conditions change, the CN becomes the table's value for the new conditions; the change is not an asset or hindrance.

A failed roll costs only the time it covered. You are still working on the same objective, and you may roll again when the next period has passed.

A 1 on either die gives the normal complication choice. Because you only know part of the language, a complication here is usually a social mishap, such as a mistranslated word that insults someone or a phrase that means something else than you meant.

## Quick Combat

Extensive rules for full combat are laid out in the next chapter. However, there are times when it is clear that your party will defeat its opponents easily, and using the full rules for combat can be more trouble than it's worth. In that case you may use quick combat, which is a stratagem (see the Stratagem System section) that settles the whole fight with up to four rolls.

If the fight is not one the party will clearly win, use the full combat rules instead.

The fight has four objectives, one for each outcome the party wants:

1. **We suffer no injury**
2. **None of our opponents escape**
3. **We get what we want**
4. **Nothing else goes wrong**

Quick combat is the exception to the Stratagem System's one objective per period: the four outcomes happen in one stretch of minutes, so every character may lead any outcome.

The GM sets one Challenge Number (CN) for the fight, representing the difficulty of the encounter.

The CN comes from the opponents:

| Opponents | CN |
| --- | --- |
| Only minions, and no more of them than there are characters in the party | 6 |
| More minions than characters, or any standard threat | 9 |
| Any elite enemy | 12 (use only with an experienced party) |
| A boss monster or legendary foe | Do not use quick combat; play the fight out |

Before the first roll, the GM lowers the CN by 3 if the party already has a clear edge (it has surprised the opponents, holds the ground, or heavily outnumbers them), and raises it by 3 if the opponents have a clear edge. Anything the party already has is counted in this step and is not also written down as an asset, so nothing is added twice. Assets and hindrances that appear after setup change the CN as usual.

The GM may also give one outcome its own CN when that part of the fight is harder, for example "none of our opponents escape" when the opponents are on horses.

Use quick combat only when the GM judges that the party clearly wins. If the GM would not bet on the party, play the fight out.

The modifiers on each roll include the rank of a profession that applies to fighting, such as soldier, guard, or hunter. Nobody helps on a quick-combat roll, as on any stratagem roll. Everyone shares a failed outcome's result, but only the leader takes the hit from a complication on outcome 1. If an outcome cannot go wrong in this fight, it counts as already finished and you do not roll for it: opponents who fight to the death cannot escape, and a party that wants nothing beyond winning cannot fail to get what it wants. Anything the party did not name as something it wants is not protected by the roll; the GM decides what happens to it from the fiction. Quick combat has no rounds, so you cannot spend stamina (Endure, Focus, or Surge) or rush points on its rolls or its damage.

Make one roll for each outcome that is not already finished. Each roll is a might, agility, or cunning check (Heart die + that ability die + modifiers). The players choose the order in which they roll the four outcomes. Each outcome may have a different player leading it.

If you roll at or above the CN, you get that outcome. If you roll below the CN, you fail that outcome, and the failure result for that outcome happens. A failure affects only the outcome you rolled for, not any other outcome. A 1 on either die gives the normal complication choice.

Before the first roll, the party states in one sentence what it wants from the fight; that is what outcome 3 is about. Playing a failed outcome out as a scene afterwards does not restore hit points already lost. When you fail an outcome or take a complication, apply the result for that outcome from this table:

| Outcome | Failure | Complication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. We suffer no injury** | Every character who fought loses hit points equal to the CN + 3. This is many small hits, so it marks no wound. | The character who led the roll takes one hit of damage equal to the CN + 3. If that reaches 10, a character's wound threshold, it marks a wound of that rank, which the GM describes as for any wound. |
| **2. None of our opponents escape** | Some opponents escape: at least one, and at most half of them rounded up. The GM chooses which. They know the party's faces and numbers and take that to whoever they answer to. If the party chases them, use the Chase rules with the fleeing opponents starting 4 spaces away. | Every opponent is stopped, but someone outside the fight saw it, such as a herder, a sentry, or a passing cart. The GM decides who that person tells. |
| **3. We get what we want** | The party does not get it. The GM says what happened to it instead, such as the captive is dead, the ledger is burned, or the strongbox is carried off. If it still exists somewhere else, getting it back can become a new objective. | The party gets it, but in worse shape than it wanted. The GM names one thing that is damaged, missing, or hurt, such as a captive who needs his wounds treated before he can talk, or half the cargo spoiled. |
| **4. Nothing else goes wrong** | One thing goes wrong now, and the GM chooses which. (a) More opponents arrive before the party can leave, and a new fight begins. The GM uses the same test to decide whether this new fight is quick or played out; finish it before rolling any remaining outcomes of the first fight. (b) A person or animal on the party's side who is not a player character, such as a hireling, a guide, someone the party protects, or a mount, takes one hit of damage equal to the CN + 3, which marks a wound if it reaches the recipient's wound threshold (10 for a character; a creature's own threshold from its stat block, and none if it has none). (c) One ordinary carried item is lost or broken, such as ammunition, a pack, a lantern, or a tool. It is never a primary weapon, worn armor, or a magic item. | The fight leaves something that causes trouble later. The GM names a hindrance (+3 CN) on the stratagem the party is pursuing; if more than one is open, the GM names which. If there is none, count it into the setup CN of the next stratagem it bears on. |

A roll with two 1s is both a failure and a complication: both results happen.
